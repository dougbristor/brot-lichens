Edge lichens · GPU trainer

Probes (a genome and a 12-cell seed) evolve on the GPU within a budget of GPU-seconds. The board is the edge of a Bristorian Julia set. ← the board and probe builder · looking for a GPU…

Board

1 · Gate: the GPU against the CPU

The GPU updates the 8 parity classes in turn; the CPU updates cells one at a time in a random order. Same rules, a different order, so the outcomes should agree in distribution. One match (a 12-cell clump with genome r 0.8, t 0.2 against plain) is played on both over the same number of seeds. Pass = every mean within 3 standard errors + 1 point. Two plants, a lineage-index bug and no recovery, must fail it.

2 · Train

A player code (from the probe builder) plays from its own seed. A cell both seeds claim stays the opponent's, so a probe cannot win by sitting on it.
Approximation knobs. Match ticks below 1500 are cheaper but less like the real match, and seeds per probe trades noise for cost. The budget is what the GPU runs took, wall clock. The best probe is always judged at the real length on held-out seeds.

While it trains · the generations play

A live game on the board (the CPU sim, the same one the gate checks). Generation 1's best plays alone on the empty edge. Each time training finds a new best, a fresh round starts: every best found so far is planted at once at its own 12-cell seed, the newest included (a cell two seeds share goes to one of them at random), and they play until the next best arrives. After training ends, the last round keeps going. A best that is the same probe as the last one does not start a round. Colour runs from the first generation (blue) to the latest (yellow); red = mould. Why rounds: landing late on a full board never works here. Births only go into empty cells, so a 12-cell seed dropped onto a full edge dies out (it held about 1% even when we cleared a 600-cell crater for it). This is a show, not a ranking: a round is one seed and often short. The ranking is section 3.

3 · Held out: the best against random probes

Real length (1500 ticks), 16 seeds never used in training, each selected opponent. Baseline: 16 random probes on the same seeds. The trained probe must beat them by more than 2 standard errors; otherwise the training learned nothing that holds.
